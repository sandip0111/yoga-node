You Practise Yoga Every Day. But Is It Truly Sadhana?

You wake up at 5 AM. You practise yoga every morning. You never miss a session.

But does that automatically mean you are practising Sadhana?

There is a difference between being disciplined in your daily yoga practice and reaching a point where your entire being naturally moves towards practice.

We often measure our progress through consistency:

  • How many years have we been practising?
  • How early do we wake up?
  • How many days have we practised without a break?
  • How advanced are our postures and techniques?

But perhaps we are overlooking a much deeper question:

What is actually changing within us through our practice?

A few things worth reflecting upon:

1. Discipline is the beginning, not necessarily the destination.

You may have practised every morning for five years, yet still find yourself fighting your mind every morning to get onto the mat.

2. Tapas and Sadhana are not exactly the same.

Effort, commitment, and discipline build the foundation. But what happens when your relationship with practice begins to change?

3. Sadhana doesn’t mean the absence of effort.

It means something deeper begins to evolve within you. Practice gradually becomes more integrated into your life.

4. Physical progress doesn’t always mean inner transformation.

You may master difficult postures, refine your breathing, and develop remarkable physical discipline. But are you becoming more attentive, aware, and understanding of yourself?

The question is:

Are you simply maintaining a daily yoga routine, or is your practice gradually transforming the way you live, think, breathe, and relate to yourself?
