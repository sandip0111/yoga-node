Namaste everyone,

How are you all. I would like to share something that has been alive in my own practice recently.

Many of us begin yoga believing that transformation comes from doing more, more pranayama, longer meditation, stronger asana, or learning increasingly advanced techniques.

But over the years, I’ve realized something different.

Your daily practice can either free you or strengthen the very patterns you are trying to overcome. Are you curious, HOW?

Whether you practise Kapalabhati, pranayama, meditation, or asana, the technique itself is not the goal.

The real practice begins when you stop chasing experiences and simply remain present.

It is your relationship with the practice that determines whether you become freer or more conditioned.

For example, during Kapalabhati, be aware of every exhalation. But once the practice ends, don’t search for a special feeling or analyse your experience. Just remain present.

That silent awareness is where transformation begins.

A few reminders for your practice:

  • Practice the technique, but don’t become attached to it.
  • Don’t measure your progress by experiences.
  • Awareness is more important than achievement.
  • Yoga is not about becoming someone else—it’s about seeing yourself clearly.

This understanding is at the heart of everything I teach.
