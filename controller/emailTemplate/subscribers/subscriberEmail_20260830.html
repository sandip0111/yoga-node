One question I often hear from people who are beginning their yoga journey is:

“Should I do a Yoga Teacher Training Course?” 🧘‍♂️

“If I complete a teacher training, does that mean I have to become a yoga teacher?”

I have been teaching and guiding students in yoga teacher training programs for more than a decade, and I want to share something that may help you look at this question differently.

A Yoga Teacher Training Course is not only about learning how to teach yoga.

For me, yoga teaching is not simply a profession or a job. It is a process of learning and transformation — first within yourself, and then, if you choose, sharing that understanding with others.

That is why I don’t believe you should join a teacher training program simply because you want a certificate or because you think you must become a teacher afterward.

You can join because you genuinely want to understand yoga more deeply and build a strong foundation for your own practice — because while videos and books can guide you, real experience builds your real foundation and provides you the environment to transform yourself.

So it is not only about wanting to become a teacher. It is about wanting to clear your doubts, understand the path, and grasp this system correctly. And that’s the reason I personally choose Yoga teachers in Yoga Vidya School.

In a comprehensive training, you may explore:

Hatha Yoga  ·  Ashtanga Vinyasa  ·  Patanjali’s Yoga Philosophy  ·  Pranayama  ·  Meditation  ·  Mantra  ·  Anatomy  ·  Ayurveda  ·  Sanskrit  ·  Yogic Texts  ·  Teaching Methodology

And after experiencing all of this, you may discover that you want to teach.

Or perhaps you may realize that your path is meditation, pranayama, philosophy, personal practice — or simply bringing yoga more deeply into your own life.

You don’t have to decide everything before you begin.

The right learning environment should help you discover your direction, not pressure you into choosing one.

Of course, today there are many Yoga Teacher Training programs available, and choosing the right one is important. Look beyond the certificate and ask yourself: Who am I learning from? What is the depth of the curriculum? And does the approach resonate with me?
