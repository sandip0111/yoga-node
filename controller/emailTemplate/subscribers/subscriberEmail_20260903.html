Dear Yoga Practitioners,

I have something interesting to share with you today.

Have you ever wondered why we are taught to inhale in one posture and exhale in another?

Most practitioners begin with simple rules:

  • Inhale and exhale deeply.
  • Breathe into the belly.
  • Inhale when you open the body, exhale when you contract.

These principles can be useful when you are beginning. But as your practice becomes deeper, you start to discover something very important:

Not every yoga posture has the same breathing pattern.

For example:

Forward Bending

Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose)

• Forward Bending:

When you move into a forward bend from standing, the exhalation naturally supports the movement. But once you remain in the posture, the quality and direction of the breath can change. The breath may begin to move more laterally, similar to what we experience in crocodile breathing.

• Backbending:

In Bhujangasana (Cobra Pose), the breath can be experienced predominantly through the abdomen. But in a posture such as Urdhva Mukha Svanasana (Upward-Facing Dog), although both are backbends, the breathing experience can become much more chest-oriented.

So the question is not simply:

“Should I inhale or exhale?”

The deeper question is:

“Why is this breath being used in this posture, and what is it doing to my body, breath, and mind?”

This is where yoga practice moves beyond simply performing physical shapes.

Asana, Pranayama, alignment and Yoga philosophy are not separate subjects. When studied together, they help us understand why a posture is practiced, why a particular breath is used, how the body adapts, and how the fluctuations of the mind gradually become quieter.

And this is also why alignment cannot be reduced to simply looking at whether a posture looks correct.

Every body is different.

And because every body is different, the experience and movement of breath can also be different.
